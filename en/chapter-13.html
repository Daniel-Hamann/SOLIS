Chapter 13

World Order: SOLIS Among the Peoples

Mara’s cousin Tomas had left ten years earlier, for an order in which one could become rich. He came to visit every summer, with presents that no one here could buy, and with a gaze that lingered on the river a little longer each year. That same summer, Mara sat as the region’s delegate in the assembly of the countries when the planetary defence reported its annual exercise: a chunk of rock two years away, three orders aiming together. None of it belonged to anyone. That was the point.

The greatest dangers of this age respect no border. Climate change, a new epidemic, a technology run wild, and a threat from space strike humanity as a whole and cannot be averted by any single country. An order conceived only inwards would therefore remain incomplete. This chapter asks how SOLIS relates to the world: how many societies work together without a new ruler arising above them, and how humanity carries what is planetary together without giving up its diversity.

The Ring at the Planetary Level

The obvious answer to global problems would be a world government, and it would be the most dangerous top of all. What holds for the individual state holds all the more for the planet. Where a highest authority arises, the highest target for capture arises, and no distance would make it safer than any throne before it. Here too, SOLIS chooses not the pyramid but nesting. The order is built in many layers, from the neighbourhood through the region and the country up to the planetary level, and each level is itself a ring: with an assembly, offices, an ethics council, courts, and a review body, partly chosen by lot, with changing membership, open to recall, transparent, without a top. Above this stands the principle of subsidiarity in its strict form. The higher level may do only what the lower one truly cannot. Only what is truly planetary is thus left to the planetary level. Everything else belongs closer to the people. Thus global coordination arises, but no global ruler.

What Is Planetary and Succeeds Only Together

Some tasks, however, only humanity as a whole can solve. The climate knows no border, a pathogen leaps over every wall, a powerful artificial intelligence in a single hand endangers everyone, and an asteroid does not distinguish between nations. For these few but decisive cases, SOLIS creates a planetary defence: shared early warning, shared capacities, shared rules. The Earth Defence of the individual orders flows together in it, and it is itself ringed in turn and belongs to no single power. Protection against the end of humanity is the one task that no sovereignty may keep for itself, and at the same time the one that no power may misuse as a pretext for its own rise. The planetary defence therefore also stands under the lot, rotation, and accountability. The commons that belong to all, the atmosphere, the oceans, the heritage of space, are managed at this level, by the same rules as the waterworks in the region.

Global Justice

A shared planet demands a shared justice. The goods that belong to everyone may not be captured by a few. Their yields benefit all of humanity, not only those who get there first. Where wealth rests on past injustice, redressing it is part of justice, not as eternal guilt but as an honest reckoning. Wealthy regions bear a particular duty, because responsibility is measured by abilities and means. And no society may found its prosperity on exploiting another. In SOLIS, diversity is a strength to learn from, not a pretext for inequality between peoples.

Belonging and Migration

Who belongs to a community, and how one joins or leaves it, touches the core of every order. SOLIS holds two things together here. On the one hand, the right to leave. No person is chained to an order, however just it may be. Whoever wants to emigrate or found a community of their own that lives differently may do so, limited only by the rule that no one may leave in order to harm others or destroy the community. This is the controlled membrane applied to people. It lets legitimate difference pass and seals only the leak through which someone would undermine the order from outside. On the other hand, the right to come. Migration is not treated as a threat but in an orderly and humane way, with responsibility towards those who seek protection as well as towards the communities that take them in. Whoever comes receives basic provision and fundamental rights from the first day, and access to language and contribution instead of to waiting rooms. Whoever takes people in receives the means to make it work. Integration is not a one-way street. Both sides learn, and diversity is respected, but not at the expense of the five foundations. An order that one may leave has to convince through what it achieves, and not by bolting the exit.

Surviving in a Mixed World

SOLIS does not arise in an empty world but alongside powers that do not share its principles. A society that binds itself ecologically and ethically while others do not can be overrun or undercut before its example takes effect. SOLIS meets this neither by retreat nor by attack, but by union: alliances of like-minded regions, mutual defence, resilient and connected supply chains, shared knowledge. Towards the outside world, it stands for a world of many poles. No bloc may dominate all others, regions and cultures remain independent and at the same time connected, and balance arises from mutual dependence, respect, and clear limits: diversity without chaos, order without subjugation. Its path outwards is the same as inwards, victory through obsolescence. Where the new order visibly serves people better, the old one loses its hold. Until then, SOLIS protects what it has built without itself becoming the aggressor.

A Humanity United, Not Uniform

The aim is neither a world state nor a patchwork, but a humanity that is united but not uniform. The five foundations apply everywhere, dignity, balance, responsibility for the planet, the advancement of knowledge and wisdom, and long-term resilience. They are the shared core, which is not negotiable. What differs is everything else: the shape of the institutions, the language of culture, the paths to meaning. As little as SOLIS prescribes a single idea of the good life, so firmly does it hold on to what all people share. A narrow, firm core and above it a rich diversity: that is the order in which humanity can act as one without ceasing to be many. Our future belongs to everyone, or it belongs to no one.
