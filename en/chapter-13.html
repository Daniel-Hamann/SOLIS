Chapter 13

World Order: SOLIS Among the Peoples

The greatest dangers of our age respect no borders. A changing climate, a new pandemic, a runaway technology, a threat from space — they strike humanity as a whole, and no single country can avert them. An order designed only for the inside would therefore remain incomplete. This chapter asks how SOLIS relates to the world: how many societies can act together without a new ruler rising above them, and how humanity can carry planetary tasks together without giving up its diversity.

The Ring at the Planetary Level

The obvious answer to global problems would be a world government — and it would be the most dangerous summit of all. What holds for a single state holds all the more for the planet: wherever a highest authority arises, so does the ultimate target for capture, and distance would make it no safer than any throne before it. Here too, SOLIS chooses not the pyramid but nesting. The order is built in many layers, from the neighbourhood through the region to the planetary level, and each layer is itself a Ring: partly chosen by lot, rotating, subject to recall, transparent, and without a summit. Above this stands the principle of subsidiarity in its strict form: a higher level may do only what the lower level genuinely cannot. The planetary level is left with only what is truly planetary; everything else belongs closer to the people. The result is global coordination, but no global ruler.

What Can Only Succeed Together

Some tasks, however, can only be solved by humanity as a whole. The climate respects no borders, a pathogen leaps every wall, a powerful artificial intelligence in a single pair of hands endangers everyone, and an asteroid does not distinguish between nations. For these few but decisive cases, SOLIS creates a Planetary Defence — shared early warning, shared capabilities, shared rules — which is itself bound into a ring and belongs to no single power. Protecting humanity from its own end is the one task that no sovereign may keep for itself, and at the same time the one that no power may use as a pretext for raising itself up. The Planetary Defence, too, is therefore subject to selection by lot, rotation, and accountability.

Global Justice

A shared planet demands shared justice. The goods that belong to everyone — the atmosphere, the oceans, the heritage of space — may not be appropriated by a few; their yields benefit all of humanity, not only those who grab them first. Where wealth rests on past wrongs, making amends is part of justice — not as eternal guilt, but as an honest reckoning. And no society may build its prosperity on exploiting another. In SOLIS, diversity is a strength to learn from, not a pretext for a hierarchy among peoples.

Belonging and Migration

Who belongs to a community, and how one joins or leaves it, touches the core of every order. SOLIS holds two things together here. On the one hand, the right to leave: no one is chained to an order, however just; anyone who wants to emigrate or found a different community of their own may do so, limited only by the rule that no one may leave in order to harm others or to destroy the common good. This is the controlled membrane applied to people: it lets legitimate difference pass and closes only the loophole through which someone would undermine the order from outside. On the other hand, the right to come: migration is treated not as a threat but as something to be managed humanely and in an orderly way, with responsibility both for those seeking protection and for the communities that receive them. An order that people are free to leave has to convince them through what it achieves, not by bolting the exit.

Surviving in a Mixed World

SOLIS does not arise in an empty world, but alongside powers that do not share its principles. A society that binds itself ecologically and ethically while others do not can be overrun or undercut before its example takes effect. SOLIS responds neither by retreating nor by attacking, but by joining with others: alliances of like-minded regions, mutual defence, resilient and networked supply chains, shared knowledge. Its path outward is the same as its path inward — winning by making the old order obsolete: where the new order visibly serves people better, the old one loses its grip. Until then, SOLIS protects what it has built without itself becoming the aggressor.

A United, Not Uniform, Humanity

The goal is neither a world state nor a patchwork, but a humanity that is united without being uniform. The five foundations apply everywhere — dignity, balance, responsibility for the planet, the advancement of knowledge and wisdom, and long-term resilience; they are the common core that is not up for negotiation. Everything else may differ: the shape of institutions, the language of culture, the paths to meaning. Just as SOLIS does not prescribe a single idea of the good life, it holds firmly to what all human beings share. A narrow, firm core with a rich diversity above it — that is the order in which humanity can act as one without ceasing to be many.
