Chapter 06

Planet, Ecology, and Regeneration

The river Mara worked on had smelled of oil when she was a child. Over twenty years, her enterprise had led it back into its old bed, bend by bend, and when the first salmon returned, the town held a festival. At every project on the banks, a steward sat at the table who spoke for the river and for those who would inherit it in a hundred years. She did not always agree. Sometimes she was right.

Everything this work designs, the Ring, the law, the economy, education, rests on a precondition that no majority can decide and no assembly can revoke: a living planet. On a destroyed Earth, the question of the best order no longer arises. Responsibility for the planet is therefore not one of the five foundations among others. It is the one without which the other four would no longer have a place. This chapter brings together what follows from it.

The Planetary Boundaries

The starting point is an insight that comes before politics because it comes from physics: the planet has limits. Science has named them planetary boundaries, the stability of the climate, the diversity of living things, the purity of water, air, and soil, and beyond these boundaries the system that sustains all life tips over. We have exhausted soils, overfished seas, cleared forests, and pumped greenhouse gases into the atmosphere as if there were no tomorrow. Species are dying at a pace that approaches the great extinction waves of the Earth’s history. We know it, we have known it for decades, and we act as if we had endless time. It follows that SOLIS treats the limits as what they are: as a wall within which all economic activity and all freedom have their space. Ecological costs are compulsorily included in every price, so that the harmful is more expensive than the gentle. Limits on extraction are not left to the market but set by the assemblies and monitored by the review body. Once something has been recognized as a limit of survival, no economic boom can lift it again.

Regeneration instead of Mere Preservation

Where too much has already been destroyed, protection is no longer enough. SOLIS therefore goes beyond preservation to restoration: not only to do less harm, but to heal actively. Forests are not only preserved but brought back. Soils are not only spared but built up. Rivers are not only less polluted but led back into their old beds and back to life. Methods that draw carbon out of the atmosphere and return it to soils and forests are part of this healing, but not a substitute for it. They stand beside avoidance, not in its place. The standard of success thus shifts from the least harm to the greatest recovery. A generation that gives back to the planet more than it takes reverses the direction in which humanity has long treated the living world.

The Closed Cycle

Nature knows no waste. What one being excretes is the food of another. SOLIS makes this principle the basic form of economic activity, and the economic order has shown how it is built into prices and into the rules for products. In place of the straight line from extraction through use to rubbish comes the closed cycle. Materials are used, reused, and recycled at high quality for as long as possible, repair comes before replacement and reuse before consumption, and whole industries are connected so that the residues of some become the raw materials of others. A disused device casing is not burned but dismantled, cleaned, and turned into new products. This goes beyond recycling. It is a design built for circulation from the start, and it also changes the people who live in it: whoever repairs instead of throwing away builds up a relationship with things again.

Yet this circle does not merely go round in circles. It keeps materials flowing and at the same time carries knowledge and prosperity upwards. A cycle in matter, progress in mind: that is the spiral in which a civilization can endure without consuming itself.

The Rights of the Living World

SOLIS widens the circle of consideration beyond human beings. An ecosystem, a river, a forest are not merely property and raw material. They carry a weight of their own that counts in decisions. Animals are sentient beings whom the order spares suffering where it can, and husbandry that turns them into mere material has no place in it. Where far-reaching projects touch the living world, it receives a voice through appointed stewards, who represent its concerns before the assemblies, the ethics councils, and the courts, just as a guardian represents the concerns of someone who cannot speak for themselves. And because the planet also belongs to future generations, every extraction is subject to the condition that those who come after still find it habitable. It follows that the ethics councils explicitly examine projects against this double claim: towards the living world and towards the people to come. An ethics that means only human beings is too small.

Agriculture and Food

Nothing connects people more closely with the planet than what they eat. Agriculture in SOLIS therefore works with nature instead of against it. Soils are built up instead of exhausted, through crop rotation, humus formation, and agroforestry, forests are managed so that their diversity remains, and fish stocks are used only to the extent that they stay stable in the long run. Factory farming, which causes suffering, devours land, and burdens the climate, is phased out. Whatever animals are kept are kept as befits beings that feel. Food is at the same time part of basic provision. Staple foods are an entitlement, and because ecological costs are included in every price, what is regional, seasonal, and gentle is the obvious choice among discretionary goods too, not the expensive luxury. Thus food begins with respect: for nature, for animals, and for one’s own body.

Energy and the Long Future

The material basis of a lasting civilization is energy that does not consume the planet. SOLIS therefore builds on renewable, decentralized, and resilient energy systems: sun, wind, water, and geothermal heat, connected by storage and intelligent grids that balance out fluctuations. Through the funds, it invests in what could lead beyond them, from fusion to storage that does not yet exist today, without building the present on promises. Fossil fuels are replaced entirely. And energy is not only produced differently but also used less. A product that lasts a lifetime, a town in which distances are short, a grid that wastes nothing save more than any plant supplies.

That supply is distributed has more than ecological reasons. A distributed supply absorbs failures that would knock down a central one. If one part fails, the others carry the load, and no single node holds the supply of society in its hand. Thus ecological necessity meets the logic of the whole work: no monopoly over a central resource, but a network that is strong in being distributed. On this basis, a planet that is recovering and an energy that does not devour it, the distant future of which this work speaks becomes possible in the first place. In doing so we are not saving “the Earth”. The Earth would manage without us. We are saving ourselves.
